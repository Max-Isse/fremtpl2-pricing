# Decision log

Judgement calls, with the alternatives considered. Numbers quoted here come from `reports/data_quality.md`
and `reports/exposure_analysis.md` (generated by `fremtpl2 report`). Re-run the pipeline before relying on
them.

## D-001 Data source and version
**Choice:** OpenML 41214 (freMTPL2freq) and 41215 (freMTPL2sev), version 1, fetched with
`sklearn.datasets.fetch_openml`. The data are cached as Parquet, and `data/raw_manifest.json` records the
SHA-256 of each file. The raw cache is never modified.
**Alternatives:** the CASdatasets R package (same data, but needs R); a Kaggle mirror (provenance less
clear).

## D-002 Exposure cap and non-positive exposure
**Choice:** cap Exposure at 1.0 year. Flag non-positive exposure and exclude it from the modelling tables.
**Why:** the portfolio is observed over one calendar year, so exposure above 1 is a recording artefact. The
cap affects 1,224 policies and removes 139 exposure-years (0.04%). No policy has non-positive exposure in
this version. The rule stays in place, and its test uses a fixture.
**Alternatives:** drop policies with exposure above 1 (loses their claims); keep them uncapped (inflates
the offset).

## D-003 Claim count = number of claim-amount records (`claim_count_source: severity_records`)
**Choice:** the modelled claim count for each policy is the number of positive amount records in
freMTPL2sev, not `ClaimNb`.
**Why:** 9,116 policies (9,657 claims, 27% of all recorded claims) have `ClaimNb > 0` but no amount
records. Of those claims, 9,639 sit in the lowest IDpol decile. That decile records 13,196 claims against
1,551 to 3,373 in every other decile, and once its claims without amounts are removed it has 3,557, in
line with the rest. This looks like a block of claim-heavy records whose amounts are absent from the
severity file, not random missingness. Counting only claims with amounts keeps frequency and severity
consistent, so frequency × severity is a valid pure premium.
**Cost:** the frequency level drops if those claims were real. The flag
`claim_count_source: recorded` rebuilds everything on `ClaimNb`, so phase 2 can report the frequency
model under both definitions.
**Alternatives:** keep `ClaimNb` for frequency and treat missing severities as missing at random (biased
if the block is special); drop the whole first IDpol block (loses 10% of policies).

## D-004 Claim count cap at 4
**Choice:** cap the per-policy claim count at 4. This affects 8 policies and removes 39 claims.
**Why:** counts of 5 to 16 in one policy year are implausible for MTPL and would dominate the Poisson
likelihood of their profile. Their severity records stay in the severity base, because they are real
amounts.
**Alternatives:** drop those policies; cap at 3 (more aggressive); no cap. Phase 2 can check the frequency
model's sensitivity.

## D-005 Large losses: cap at the 99.5th percentile and spread the excess as a flat loading
**Choice:** cap each claim at the 0.995 quantile of training claim amounts (32,162). The excess is added
back to every premium as a flat proportional loading on capped claim cost (37.8% on training data).
**Why:** a few claims dominate total cost. The largest is 4.08 million, and the amount above the cap on just
106 training claims is 27% of all training losses. Fitting severity factors on uncapped data would turn a handful of claims into rating
relativities. The loading keeps the total portfolio premium adequate.
**Sensitivity:** cap at q=0.99 gives a 47% loading; q=0.999 gives 21% (`reports/data_quality.md` §6).
The loading is large under every reasonable cap, which is itself a finding: in this book the large-loss
load is a material, non-risk-differentiated share of the premium.
**Threshold estimated on training claims only**, so the holdout does not inform it.
**Alternatives:** fit a Pareto/GPD tail and price the excess by exposure (better for reinsurance-style
pricing, but more machinery than this portfolio supports); spread the excess by segment (re-introduces the
noise the cap removes); remove large claims (understates cost).

## D-006 Near-duplicate policies define the split groups
**Choice:** rows with identical values for all nine rating factors share a `group_id`. The holdout and the
CV folds are drawn by group.
**Why:** 257,911 rows (38%) belong to groups of two or more. These are not independent customers. The
median IDpol span within a group is 2, and in 94% of groups the total exposure is at most one year. That
pattern looks like one policy split into several records (e.g. mid-term changes). A row-level split would
put parts of the same policy on both sides, so the model would be scored on records it effectively
trained on.
**Alternatives:** row-level random split (leaky); collapsing groups into one row (loses the exposure
structure, and genuinely different customers can share a profile).

## D-007 Validation design without dates
**Choice:** 20% grouped holdout, never used before final evaluation, plus 5 grouped folds on the
remaining 80%, with seed 20261029. Bootstrap CIs on key metrics (phase 4).
**Why:** there are no policy dates, so out-of-time validation is impossible. The holdout plays the role of
"next year's book", but it cannot reveal drift or rate-change effects. That limitation is stated in the
model card.
**Alternatives:** IDpol order as a pseudo-time index. Rejected because IDpol is not documented as
chronological, and D-003 shows its first block is structurally different.

## D-008 Exposure enters frequency models as an offset
**Choice:** use log(exposure) as an offset (coefficient fixed at 1), never as a free feature, even though
the free coefficient fits better (0.67, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.69). See `docs/methodology.md` §1.
**Alternatives:** free coefficient (better deviance, but not a filable pricing rule); ignoring exposure
(halves the implied annual frequency).

## D-009 GLM factor banding and reference levels
**Choice:** band DrivAge, VehAge, VehPower, BonusMalus and log(Density) at the edges in `configs/config.yaml`
(`glm.bands`), with left-closed bands and an open-ended top band. VehBrand, VehGas and Region stay as given. The
reference level of each factor is its largest-exposure level. Area is excluded.
**Why:** banding gives one filable relativity per level. The edges follow the exposure tables, and every band has
at least 1,450 training exposure-years (the smallest is BonusMalus 115+). Region keeps all 22 levels, some of them
thin; see phase 5. Area's boundaries are nested cuts of Density (A ≤ 50, …, F ≥ 10,008 inhabitants/km²), and given
log-density bands it adds nothing (add-one LR p = 0.093, quasi-F p = 0.19).
**Alternatives:** splines or continuous log(BonusMalus) (smoother, but harder to file and to compare with the
tariff structure); Area instead of Density (coarser: 6 levels vs 8).

## D-010 Poisson mean, cluster-robust inference
**Choice:** fit the frequency tariff by Poisson maximum likelihood. Report Wald CIs and tests from a cluster-robust
(sandwich) covariance with profile groups as clusters. Fit NB2 for comparison, not as the tariff.
**Why:** with the exposure-band control, overdispersion is real but modest. Pearson χ²/df is 1.27, Cameron–Trivedi
gives α = 0.71 (p = 0.0009) and NB2 gives α = 0.73. NB2 relativities differ from Poisson by at most 1.8%, so the
tariff hardly changes. Cluster-robust SEs don't depend on a variance assumption and also cover correlation between
split records of one policy. They average 3.5% above the model-based SEs, against +2.3% for NB2 and +12.6% for
quasi-Poisson.
**Alternatives:** NB2 as the tariff model (nearly the same means, extra parameter, harder to deploy); quasi-Poisson
(a single inflation factor for every coefficient).

## D-011 AgeMalus interaction
**Choice:** add a derived factor with levels `base`, `18-30 & malus` and `61+ & malus` (malus = BonusMalus > 50).
A malus at ages 31–60 stays in `base`, which keeps the factor identifiable alongside the BonusMalus bands.
**Why:** out-of-fold A/E on segments outside the model showed older drivers with a malus under-predicted (up to
1.33) and older drivers at BM=50 over-predicted. The factor brings these segments to 0.96–1.06. It is strongly
significant (LR χ² = 113, 2 df; relativities 0.73 and 1.52), improves CV deviance in 5/5 folds, and has a
plain-language reading: a malus says more about recent claims for an experienced driver than for a young one.
**Alternatives:** a full DrivAge × BonusMalus interaction (dozens of thin cells, unstable); leaving it to the GBM
(the GLM tariff would then knowingly misprice older drivers with claims).

## D-012 Exposure-band control, priced at full year (adopted 2026-10-29)
**Choice:** fit the frequency GLM with an exposure-band control factor (`glm.controls.ExposureBand`) alongside the
log(exposure) offset. When pricing (`GLMDesign.matrix(..., pricing=True)`), pin the control at the full-year band,
so every policy gets the full-year annual rate, charged pro rata to time on cover.
**Why:** with the offset alone, the short-record effect (methodology §1) leaks into the base rate and
relativities. Full-year records, which are what is quoted, come out at out-of-fold A/E 0.82, i.e. overcharged by
about 18%. With the control, full-year A/E is 1.000 (95% CI 0.977–1.023). Removing the control would move the base
frequency by 14% and some BonusMalus and Region relativities by up to 17%. Out-of-fold deviance also improves
(5.8% vs 5.0% explained).
**Consequence, carried into phase 6:** charging every record pro rata at the full-year rate funds 16,542 of
21,152 training claims (book A/E 1.28), because short records claim more per year. That excess has to be funded
by a separate, explicit mechanism, such as a short-period or cancellation rate scale or a book-level loading.
Phase 6 makes it a configurable loading with sensitivity analysis. Option A hid the same cost inside every
full-year premium.
**Alternatives considered:** option A (offset only), rejected for the full-year bias; a free log(exposure)
coefficient, rejected because it is a non-pro-rata rule (D-008).

## D-013 Severity: coarse bands, selection by grouped CV (paired one-SE rule)
**Choice:** use severity-specific coarse bands (`severity.bands`). Run backward elimination by quasi-F to order
the factors, then choose the smallest model on that path whose grouped-CV Gamma deviance is within one *paired*
standard error of the best. The result is **VehAge** plus the exposure-band control.
**Why:** severity has about 27× less data and far noisier outcomes than frequency. In-sample F-tests at 5% would
keep five factors, including Region with 21 levels, but that model is worse out of fold than smaller ones. The
paired SE is used because fold-to-fold noise is shared by all models; unpaired SEs (0.024) are ten times the
differences between models and would always choose the intercept.
**Near miss:** {DrivAge, VehAge} has the lowest mean CV deviance, but its gain over VehAge alone (0.0011) is
within its paired SE (0.0015). BonusMalus is borderline in-sample (p = 0.04).
**Alternatives:** F-test selection (overfits here); severity using the same factors as frequency (what an
unconstrained Tweedie GLM effectively does); penalised GLM (lasso). The penalised GLM is a reasonable phase 4
option but harder to file.

## D-014 Exposure-band control in the severity model
**Choice:** fit severity with the same exposure-band control as frequency (`severity.controls`) and pin it at
full year when pricing.
**Why:** mean capped severity falls from 2,512 (records under one month) to 1,369 (full-year records). This is
the strongest severity signal in the data (quasi-F 39.7). Without the control, the severity base level is an
average over durations, and frequency × severity over-prices full-year records by about 18%. With it, full-year
cost A/E is 1.005.
**Consequence:** the short-record cost gap identified in D-012 grows: pro-rata at full-year rates funds 64% of
capped losses. Phase 6 must load it explicitly.

## D-015 Pure-premium route: frequency × severity, with a Tweedie GLM as cross-check
**Choice:** the tariff is frequency GLM × severity GLM × (1 + large-loss loading). A Tweedie GLM (power from the
severity dispersion, p = 1.76; intercept rebalanced to actual losses) is fitted as a cross-check only.
**Why:** frequency × severity is balanced by level and calibrated on full-year records (A/E 1.005), and each
component has its own validated factor set. The Tweedie GLM is not balanced (A/E 0.967 before correction) and,
after rebalancing, under-prices full-year records by 11%. It also lets frequency factors carry severity effects
that do not generalise (D-013).
**Alternatives:** Tweedie as the tariff (one model, but the calibration issues above); a Tweedie GLM with p
estimated by profile likelihood (sensitivity to ±0.1 in p is small: 5th–95th percentile premium ratio
0.97–1.03).

## D-016 Severity distribution for simulation: empirical, not Gamma
**Choice:** the phase 6 Monte Carlo resamples empirical capped claim amounts (by exposure band) instead of
simulating from the fitted Gamma.
**Why:** 41% of claims sit on four flat-rate amounts, and Gamma quantile residuals have mean 0.38 and SD 0.54
instead of 0 and 1. The Gamma variance function fits (slope 2.41, CI 1.92–2.89), but the distribution shape does
not. Using the fitted Gamma would misstate profit volatility and tail risk (VaR/TVaR).

## D-017 (OPEN, phase 6) Split the large-loss loading by record duration
**Issue:** the flat large-loss loading (D-005, 37.8%) is a book average. Excess above the cap is 11.6% of capped
losses on full-year records but 48.2% on shorter records (large losses are 0.29% vs 0.62% of claims).
**Plan:** in phase 6, load the full-year rate with the full-year excess ratio and move the rest into the explicit
short-record loading, so full-year customers are not charged for large losses that concentrate on short records.
Both ratios go into config with sensitivity analysis.

## D-018 Tree benchmarks: same information, same exposure treatment, grouped-CV tuning
**Choice:**
- *Features:* LightGBM and the random forest see the GLM's rating factors in raw form (DrivAge, VehAge, VehPower,
  BonusMalus, log Density, VehBrand, VehGas, Region; Area excluded as in D-009) plus the exposure band as an
  ordinal control feature, pinned at full year when pricing.
- *Exposure:* frequency uses log(exposure) as init_score; Tweedie uses exposure weights.
- *Tuning:* LightGBM is tuned by a seeded random search (6 configurations per model, `benchmarks.lgbm` in config)
  with grouped 5-fold CV and early stopping. The final model is refit on the full training split with the mean
  best iteration count.
- *Random forest:* Poisson criterion with regularised defaults, not tuned.

**Why:** this gives a like-for-like comparison. Any gain comes from functional form (interactions,
non-linearity), not from extra information or a different exposure treatment.
**Alternatives:** a larger search (diminishing returns: the six frequency configurations span only 0.2334–0.2342 in
CV deviance); monotone constraints on BonusMalus (a phase 5 candidate); exposure as a free feature (rejected for the
same reason as D-008).

## D-019 (OPEN, phases 5–6) Which model sets prices
**Evidence so far:** on the holdout, LightGBM beats the GLM on frequency deviance, on the pure-premium Gini (all
records +0.043, full-year records +0.070, both CIs above 0) and on the double lift, and is calibrated on full-year
records (A/E 1.006 vs 1.037).
**Against it, to be assessed:**
1. Priced premiums differ by more than 25% for 55% of policies, concentrated in segments that are almost never
   full-year (B12, new vehicles, BonusMalus 100+), where neither model's full-year premium can be validated.
2. LightGBM moves short-record cost into rating factors.
3. Explainability, stability and monotonicity, to be checked in phase 5.
4. Customer impact under rate caps and elasticity, to be checked in phase 6.

**Decision deferred** to the end of phase 6, when the pricing report weighs accuracy against these.

## D-020 LightGBM Tweedie is not calibrated in total
**Finding:** LightGBM with the Tweedie objective under-predicts total holdout capped losses by 11% (fitted A/E
1.127). The Tweedie GLM needed the same base-level correction in phase 3 (D-015). Its ranking is good (Gini 0.329),
but a level correction would be required before any use. It is kept as a benchmark only; LightGBM frequency ×
severity is the tree candidate in D-019.

## D-021 No external socioeconomic data in the fairness review
**Choice:** the proxy-risk review uses only the dataset (geography's share of premium, dispersion vs experience,
cost and cross-subsidy of removing it). No INSEE regional statistics were added.
**Why:** the brief allows external data only if the match to the dataset's pre-2016 region codes can be verified.
The codes look like INSEE region codes (R11 = Île-de-France, …), but nothing in the dataset documents that, and
an unverified join would put fabricated precision into a fairness finding.
**What would change it:** a documented code list from the data's publisher, then median income by region from
INSEE, to test whether premiums rise with deprivation after controlling for claims experience.

## D-022 (proposal for phase 6) Drop Region from the GLM tariff, keep density with monitoring
**Evidence:** removing Region costs 0.001 of CV Gini and 0.0003 of CV deviance. Region carries 5% of GLM
premium variance through 22 levels, several of them thin (R43: 445 exposure-years, relativity CI 0.57–1.24),
with the largest refit-to-refit swings of any factor (up to 20%). Density carries most of the geographic risk
signal (removing both costs 0.017 Gini), and its effect is monotone and tracks observed loss cost.
**Proposal:** phase 6 simulates a GLM tariff without Region as one of the strategies, so the commercial effect
(who moves, retention, loss ratio) can be weighed before the final recommendation. Alternative: keep Region but
shrink thin levels towards the book (credibility weighting).
**Related:** the GLM's non-monotone BonusMalus scale (58–67 above 68–79) would be smoothed in a filed version,
e.g. by merging the two bands; phase 6 uses the fitted scale and the pricing report flags it.

## D-023 Simulation basis: revenue-neutral strategies on the holdout book, renewing from a BonusMalus-only tariff
**Choice:** each strategy is scaled to collect the same total premium from the full holdout book (true cost /
(1 − expenses − margin)). The "current" premium customers renew from is a BonusMalus-only tariff scaled to the
same total.
**Why:** revenue neutrality isolates the effect of *how* premium is spread, which is what rating-structure choice
changes. A bonus-malus-only tariff is the simplest credible status quo for French MTPL, and it is the phase 2
baseline.
**Alternatives:** the flat rate as status quo (exaggerates every change); each strategy at its own level
(confounds structure with rate level).

## D-024 "True" cost = calibrated blend of GLM and LightGBM; realised losses as the model-free check
**Choice:** true expected cost is the geometric mean of the two models' fitted costs, calibrated to actual total
holdout losses. Competitors charge that cost / (1 − 30%). Results are reported on expected (model) cost *and* on
actual holdout losses weighted by retention.
**Why:** using either model as truth would make that model win by construction. Truth = GLM and truth = LightGBM
are run as sensitivities.

## D-025 Retention: an assumed logistic elasticity model (not fitted)
**Choice:** logit P(renew) = logit(0.85) − 3·log(new/current) − 4·log(new/competitor), scaled ×0.5 and ×2 in
sensitivity.
**Why:** there is no price or lapse data. The form is the standard renewal-elasticity shape: customers react both
to their own price change and to the market price. The values are plausible orders of magnitude, not estimates.
Any real use needs a price test (randomised price changes on a sample of renewals) to estimate them.

## D-026 Strategy of own design: a filable GLM with a ±15% cap
**Choice:** the GLM refitted without Region (D-022), with BonusMalus bands 58–67 and 68–79 merged so the malus
scale is monotone, priced with its own short-period scale, and changes capped at ±15% of the current premium.
**Why:** this answers the phase 5 objections (thin, unstable geography levels; a non-monotone malus scale) while
keeping the GLM's explainability. **Cost:** realised profit 0.19M vs 0.26M for the capped full GLM, about 0.5% of
premium, which is well within one year's noise (SD about 1.5M).

**D-017 resolved:** the large-loss loading is split into full-year (11.6%) and part-year (48.2%) records.
**D-012 / D-014 consequence resolved:** short-record cost is charged through a short-period scale by exposure band
(each model's fitted / priced cost on training), not loaded onto full-year customers.

## D-027 Deployed tariff: the GLM frequency x severity tariff, packaged as one versioned artefact
**Choice:** `fremtpl2 package` writes `artifacts/tariff.json`, the single input to batch scoring and the API.
It contains the frequency and severity GLMs, the short-period factors by exposure band, the full-year and
part-year large-loss loadings, the expense and margin basis and the cap. The model version is
`<model name>-<first 12 hex characters of the SHA-256 of that content>`, so any change to coefficients or
assumptions changes the version, and every quote carries it.
**Why the GLM:** in the phase 6 simulation, the GLM tariff (with a cap) gave the most robust profit, and phases
4–5 showed LightGBM's premiums are far less stable per policy. D-019 is formally closed in the phase 8 pricing
report; the scoring code is model-agnostic at the artefact level, so a filable variant (D-026) can be packaged
the same way.
**Quote-time cap:** with `current_premium`, the term premium is clipped to ±cap. Unlike the simulation, there is
no revenue-neutral rescaling at quote time; the book-level effect of the cap must be monitored (monitoring plan).
**Caveat — short-period factors:** the data-driven factors (e.g. 2.15 for a 3–6-month term) reflect freMTPL2's
record structure (split records, early terminations). Before real use they need business review against the
insurer's cancellation and short-term rules; full-year quotes are unaffected.
**Not built:** the optional Streamlit app (the API and batch scoring cover quoting; strategy comparison lives in
`reports/strategy_simulation.md`).

## D-019 closed: the GLM sets prices; LightGBM is a shadow challenger
**Decision:** file the frequency × severity GLM (filable variant, D-026) with changes capped at ±15%. LightGBM runs
as a challenger and diagnostic tool.
**Why:** LightGBM ranks risk better on the holdout, but its advantage does not survive caps and the assumed
elasticity (capped realised profit 0.04M vs 0.26M for the GLM), its policy premiums move 20% between refits
(GLM 7.5%), and its largest premium differences sit in profiles almost never observed at full year
(`reports/pricing_report.md`).

## D-022 closed: Region dropped in the filed tariff, density kept and monitored
**Decision:** the recommended (filable) tariff drops Region; density stays, with an annual fairness check
(monitoring plan item 12). Estimated cost 0.06M a year realised profit vs keeping Region (0.5% of premium).

## D-028 Deployed variant is configurable; the default is the recommended filable GLM
**Choice:** `scoring.variant: filable` makes `fremtpl2 package` refit the frequency GLM without the dropped factors
and with the merged BonusMalus bands (same specification as the phase 6 strategy), recompute its short-period
factors, and package it. `full` packages the phase 2 GLM unchanged. The variant is part of the hashed tariff, so
the model version distinguishes them.
